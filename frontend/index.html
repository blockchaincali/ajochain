Save in a circle.
The contract holds the pot.

Ajo, esusu, adashe: the savings circle you already know. Everyone pays in each round, one member takes the whole pot, and it rotates until everyone has had a turn. AjoChain runs the circle on Mezo in MUSD, a dollar stablecoin backed by Bitcoin, so nobody has to trust a collector and your savings hold their dollar value.

How a circle runs

Five steps, all enforced by the contract. No admin can move the money.

  1. Create

    Set the contribution, how many members, and how long each round lasts.

  2. Join

    Lock one contribution as collateral. The circle starts the moment the last seat fills, and the collateral starts earning.

  3. Pay each round

    When everyone has paid, the full pot goes straight to that round's member.

  4. Close late rounds

    After the deadline anyone can close the round. A late member's collateral fills the gap.

  5. Earn your badge

    Finish without missing a round: collateral comes back with BTC earnings, and you get a soulbound AjoBadge.

What changes when the circle is on-chain

No collector to run off
The pot sits in a contract, not in someone's account. It can only go to the member whose turn it is.
Defaults don't break the circle
Every member locks one contribution up front. If someone misses a round, their collateral pays it, and the recipient still gets the full pot.
A savings record you own
Each clean cycle mints a non-transferable AjoBadge to your wallet: proof you saved and paid on time, readable by any app.
Savings in a Bitcoin-backed dollar
Circles save in MUSD, Mezo's stablecoin backed by Bitcoin and pegged to the US dollar, so a pot is worth the same when it pays out as when you saved it. MEZO circles work too.
Collateral that earns
Instead of sitting idle for the whole cycle, MUSD collateral goes into Mezo's Stability Pool, which pays depositors BTC from liquidated loans. At the end you get your MUSD back plus your share of the BTC.

    Questions

    Who decides the payout order?

    The order people join. The first member to join receives the first pot, and so on. Everyone can see the order before the circle starts.

    What happens if I miss a round?

    Once the round's deadline passes, anyone can close it. Your locked collateral covers your payment, so the recipient still gets the full pot. You're marked as having missed a round and won't receive a badge for that circle.

    How does my collateral earn?

    If the circle has “Earn while you save” on, the contract deposits everyone's MUSD collateral into Mezo's Stability Pool when the circle starts. The pool uses deposits to repay loans that get liquidated and hands the depositors the BTC that backed those loans, usually worth more than the MUSD used. At the end of the cycle you get your MUSD back and claim your share of the BTC. Liquidations can shrink the MUSD part slightly while the BTC part grows.

    What if the Stability Pool is busy?

    Mezo briefly blocks Stability Pool withdrawals while an under-collateralized loan is waiting to be liquidated. A late round can be closed again a few minutes later. The final payout is never blocked: if refunds can't leave the pool yet, anyone can press “Return collateral” once it reopens.

    When do I get my collateral back?

    Automatically, in the same transaction that pays out the final round, together with your share of any BTC earned. If your collateral was used to cover a missed payment, there's nothing left to return.

    Is this real money?

    Not yet. AjoChain runs on the Mezo testnet. Get free test BTC from the Mezo faucet, then borrow test MUSD against it in the Mezo testnet app.

    Why do I need gas tokens?

    Every action is a blockchain transaction. On Mezo, gas is paid in BTC. The Mezo faucet gives out free test BTC.
